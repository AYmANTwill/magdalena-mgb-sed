1. **A decision is recorded in the doc that takes it, and the doc that *asked* the question is
   never told.** `docs/30` §1 records that the advisor declined — `docs/21` §5 and `docs/24`

   that the CHIRPS refit was a no-op — `docs/18`'s *own* §8 item 20 still says "not attempted".
   Citation travels forward; outcome does not travel back.
2. **Selective back-annotation is worse than none.** `docs/24` had slides 8 and 9 corrected on
   2026-08-10 and slides 15–17 left alone; `docs/26` had an addendum appended and §7 left alone;
   `docs/00_INDEX.md` §4 was corrected during this very sweep while §3, §6 and §7 were not. A
   partially-updated document reads as a fully-updated one.

The cheapest durable fix is a rule, not 44 edits: **a doc that closes a question must edit every
doc that cites it as open**, and the closing doc's own back-annotation must name them (which
`docs/30`'s 2026-08-12 note does — it explicitly names `docs/31` B1 as the twin it could not
touch). That single convention would have caught 11 of the 15 HIGH rows.
