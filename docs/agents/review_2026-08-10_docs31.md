### F3 — C4.2 judges a log-flux KGE against a band published for SSC (concentration) KGE. DISTORTS-A-RESULT

**(a) The two statements.**

> `docs/31` C4.2:
> "objective = KGE on **log flux** (flux spans decades) at the C1-usable tributary set […]
> success = median log-flux KGE within Fagundes' −0.26…0.44 band at calibration stations"

> `docs/19` §3.9(c) and §6:
> "**Fagundes et al. (2026) report SSC KGE from −0.26 to 0.44** — with in-situ data and 25 years
> of calibration." … "| **Realistic performance bar** | **SSC KGE −0.26 … 0.44** |"

> `docs/13` §B:
> "MGB-SED outputs suspended-sediment **concentration**, and our observations are **concentration
> (kg/m³)** too. So the model can be validated by **direct concentration comparison at every
> sediment station** — no discharge needed at that station."

**(b) Why it misdirects.** Three transformations separate the plan's statistic from the
literature band: concentration → flux (multiplying by Q, which the model also simulates),
linear → log, and per-station → fleet median. KGE on a log-transformed variable is
systematically higher than on the raw variable, so the criterion is likely to be met by
construction — and docs/19 §3.9(c) is explicit that this is the failure mode to fear:
"**a reported SSC KGE above ~0.5 should be treated as a bug until proven otherwise**".
The project has already been burned by exactly this class of mismatch and recorded it
(docs/26 §6: *"passing or failing them on this one is not a like-for-like test of the
pre-registered number"*; docs/21 §4 item 11 carries it as an open item).

A second consequence rides along: moving the target from concentration to flux silently shrinks
the usable station pool from 79 (concentration comparison, docs/13 §B) to the **33** stations
with paired discharge / **24** calibration-safe. The plan justifies dropping *yields* via the
docs/23 area embargo; it never justifies dropping *concentration*.

**(c) Severity.** DISTORTS-A-RESULT — this is the pass/fail verdict of the entire sediment
calibration.

**(d) Smallest fix.** In C4.2, register the like-for-like criterion as **KGE on SSC
(concentration, untransformed)** at calibration stations against the −0.26…0.44 band, and keep
the log-flux KGE as a named, explicitly non-comparable secondary diagnostic.

### F4 — C5.3 attributes the contrast on a *difference* of unequal-length window totals; C2.1's comparability rule forbids only *ratios*. DISTORTS-A-RESULT

**(a) The two statements.**

> `docs/31` C2.1:
> "**Comparability rule (hard):** the primary windows have unequal lengths (12 vs 24 months).
> Cross-window comparisons therefore use RATES ONLY — t/day (and monthly means). Window totals
> (t) may be reported per window for context but **NEVER as a wet:dry ratio; a ratio of unequal-
> window totals is meaningless by construction.**"

> `docs/31` C5.3:
> "Per-minibacia erosion **difference** map (2011 − 2015/16); ranked sub-basin **contributions
> to the outlet flux difference**."

**(b) Why it misdirects.** A difference of totals is exactly as length-dependent as a ratio; the
rule's letter closes one and leaves the other open, and C5.3 builds the project's headline
spatial deliverable on the open one. Concretely: at a minibacia where La Niña 2011 erodes at
2.0 t/day and El Niño at 1.2 t/day, the rate contrast is correctly wet-dominated (2.0 vs 1.2),
but the totals are 730 t vs **877 t** — the map pixel flips sign and the sub-basin ranking
reorders. The sign of the ENSO contrast is the project's result; a length artefact that inverts
it per minibacia is the worst place for this to land. C5.2's "magnitude ratio" is likewise
unqualified.

Audit of every other place flux ratios appear: **C2.2 respects the rule** ("the wet:dry ratio
*computed on rates per the C2.1 comparability rule*; window totals (t) as context only");
**C2.4 respects it** (annual flux, a rate); the **risk register respects it** (no ratio in any
row). C5 is the only breach, and it is the deliverable.

**(c) Severity.** DISTORTS-A-RESULT.

**(d) Smallest fix.** In C2.1 replace "NEVER as a wet:dry ratio" with "never in **any**
cross-window comparison — ratio **or difference**", and add to C5.2/C5.3: "all cross-window
quantities are rates (t/day, or per-month means) per C2.1".

### F10 — C0.5's `Qsur` is ambiguous between the engine's `d_sup` and `q_sup`, and C3.4's own unit test is satisfiable by only one of them. DISTORTS-A-RESULT

**(a) The statements.**

> `docs/31` C0.5: "Store per-minibacia daily fields the sediment model needs: **surface runoff
> `Qsur` (mm/day)**, total reach inflow, and the chosen peak proxy input"
> `docs/31` C3.4 test list: "zero-rain ⇒ zero erosion"

The engine holds both quantities under distinct names, and neither is `Qsur`
(`src/mgb_hydrology.py`):

- generation — line 38 `Dsup = Pthr * Asat`; line 96 "``Dsup/Dint/Dbas``, ``P``, ``PET`` . mm/day"
- routed outflow — line 727 `q_sup = st.s_sup * ex.c_sup` (the surface linear reservoir)
- both are serialised side by side — line 939
  `keys = ("p", "et", "d_sup", "d_int", "d_bas", "q_sup", "q_int", "q_bas", …)`

**(b) Why it misdirects.** MUSLE's `Q_surf` is the hillslope surface-runoff **volume**, i.e.
`d_sup`. If a session stores `q_sup`, C3.4's "zero-rain ⇒ zero erosion" test **fails by
construction** — the reservoir keeps draining on dry days — and the session will be debugging its
own MUSLE implementation against a driver defect. The two series are not close: with the adopted
H2E's railed `k_sup = 19.199 d` (F2), `q_sup` is `d_sup` smoothed over ~19 days, which is
precisely the peak damping C3.3 is written to bound. The plan names neither variable and warns
about neither.

**(c) Severity.** DISTORTS-A-RESULT.

**(d) Smallest fix.** C0.5: "surface runoff = the engine's **`d_sup`** (generation, mm/day) —
**not** `q_sup`, the surface-reservoir outflow, which with H2E's railed `k_sup ≈ 19 d` is a
~19-day smoothing of it and would break C3.4's zero-rain test."

## 4 — Verdict

**The plan is not safe to execute as written, but it is close** — its failures are of omission
and of citation, not of design. The architecture is sound: pointer-justified stages, pre-registered
decision rules, gates that can fail in both directions, and a risk register that anticipates most
of what will go wrong. What it does not do is carry the *sediment* network's measured limits into
its own ground truth. Four of the twelve findings (F1, F6, F7, and the pool consequence inside F3)
are the same omission seen from different angles: docs/31 §0 describes a 79-station fleet, while
`sediment_inventory.csv` and docs/19 describe 28 mapped / 24 calibration-safe / **6 bridging**
stations with a one-sided mainstem anchor and no observed basin export. A session that opens
C1 or C2 on §0's numbers will build the right machinery for the wrong-sized problem, and will
discover it three subtasks in.

**Fix stage C1 first — specifically the §0 ground-truth table it reads from.** It is the cheapest
edit with the widest blast radius: correcting the mapping row (F1), adding the bridging-station
row (F6), and adding the H2E railing row (F2) repairs the premises of C1, C2, C4 and C5 in one
pass, before any code is written. Do it together with F5 (the phantom docs/21 citation) and F4
(extend the comparability rule from ratios to differences), since both are one-line edits to
prose that a later stage will otherwise inherit as settled. F9 and F10 are one-line edits to C0
and should ride along, because C0 runs first and F10 in particular would surface as a
mysterious unit-test failure deep inside C3.4. F3 — scoring against the Fagundes band with a
metric it was not published in — is the one finding that should be re-registered rather than
edited in passing: it decides whether the project declares success, and this repository's own
history (docs/26 §6) is that a benchmark mismatch discovered after the fact costs a criterion.
