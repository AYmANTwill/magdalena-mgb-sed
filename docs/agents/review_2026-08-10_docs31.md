### F8 · DISTORTS-A-RESULT — C3.3's option-1 `qpeak` formula is dimensionally inconsistent with the MUSLE it feeds, and the error is area-dependent

**(a) The clashing statements.**

docs/31 C3.3:
> "1. `qpeak = Qsur/86400` (daily mean as peak — floor estimate);"

docs/31 C0.5:
> "surface runoff `Qsur` (**mm/day**)"

`src/mgb_hydrology.py:660`:
> `"""One day of the URH column balance.  Returns (Dsup, Dint, Dbas, ET) in **mm/day**."""`

docs/31 C3 goal line: `Sed = α·(Qsur·qpeak·A)^β · K · C · P · LS2D`, with the α = 11.8,
β = 0.56 Williams (1975) defaults from §0 — a MUSLE whose q_peak argument is **m³/s**.

`Qsur[mm/day] / 86400` is **mm/s**. The area conversion is missing: the correct floor estimate
is `qpeak = Qsur[mm] · A[km²] / 86.4` (m³/s).

**(b) Why it misdirects.** The error is not a constant. It is a factor of `A/86.4 × 10³`, so it
varies across the minibacia size range by orders of magnitude — which means it is **not**
absorbable into a global α at calibration. It silently re-weights the spatial pattern that C5.3
exists to report, and C4's α/β would fit around it. C3.4's stated protection —
*"units audit (a hand-computed single-cell case matches to 1e-12)"* — reproduces the plan's own
expression and passes.

**(c) Severity:** DISTORTS-A-RESULT.

**(d) Smallest fix.** Write option 1 as `qpeak = Qsur·A/86.4` with units annotated
(`Qsur` mm/day, `A` km², `qpeak` m³/s), and make C3.4's units audit an independent dimensional
assertion on the MUSLE argument rather than a re-derivation of the plan's formula.

### F12 · DISTORTS-A-RESULT — C3.1's LS2D equation is attributed to a paper it does not come from, and its two exponents are the only unregistered free parameters in the plan

**(a) The choice, quoted.** docs/31 C3.1:
> "Desmet & Govers (1996) two-dimensional LS from the conditioned DEM … per cell
> `LS = (m+1)·(A/22.13)^m·(sin β/0.0896)^n`, aggregated area-weighted per URH per minibacia."

Three problems, in decreasing confidence:

1. **`m` and `n` are never given.** Everything else load-bearing in this plan is pre-registered —
   the qpeak choice (C3.3), the C/P values with "a source column per value" (C3.2), α/β bounds
   "registered exactly at write-time" (C4.2), the calibration cells, the factor-swap list
   (C5.3). LS enters MUSLE multiplicatively **per cell**, so `m` and `n` set the spatial pattern
   C5.3 is built to report. This is the plan's own discipline applied everywhere except here.
   *(This part rests on repo evidence alone: `grep` over docs/31 finds no `m =` or `n =`.)*
2. **`A` is undefined.** In the quoted form, `22.13 m` is the USLE unit-plot *slope length*, so
   `A` must be the upslope area **per unit contour width** (m²/m). The nb07 chain's product is a
   D8 flow *accumulation* (see F7). Using accumulated area directly inflates every LS by
   `(cell width)^m`; C3.1's own sanity gate ("basin median in the literature range … ~2–10") is
   the only thing that would catch it, and only if the session reads it as a hard gate.
3. **The attribution.** The quoted expression is the Moore & Burch (1986) unit-stream-power form
   (as popularised by Mitasova et al. 1996); Desmet & Govers (1996) is a different,
   finite-difference `L` computed from flow-accumulation in/out per cell and the cell size. I
   flag this from the literature, not from the repo — the paper is not in `data/` or `docs/` —
   so the executing session should verify it against both sources rather than take it from me.
   The concrete risk stands either way: a session that follows the citation and a session that
   follows the formula implement two different things.

**(b) Severity:** DISTORTS-A-RESULT.

**(c) Smallest fix.** Register `m` and `n` with their source in C3.1's text, state
`A = upslope area / contour width [m]`, and either correct the citation to the form actually
written or switch to the Desmet–Govers formulation and say which.
